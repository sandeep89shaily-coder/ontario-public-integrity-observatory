## Why these two smaller Brampton nonprofits are selected

This is a direct **dual-grantee affiliation** question. Two separate nonprofit names both received City of Brampton Arts & Culture ABF awards, **each $25,000 in 2024**, and separately reappear in 2025 and 2026. Unlike conjectural organizational links, (a) the Canadian federal registry names **Jay Bhavsar** as a director of Canadian Desi Settlement Services, and (b) Swar Sadhana Music Lovers Club's own current website names **Jay Bhavsar as president**. Their public records also show the *same published contact phone numbers*, and Swar Sadhana's website gives the *same Brampton registered-office location* as CDSS's federal registry. The CDSS website's membership section **uses both the CDSS name and SSMLC as the membership-program operator**, and promotes tickets to Swar Sadhana productions. These are meaningful grounds to investigate **how project scope and funds were separated**, not evidence that either grant application or invoice was fraudulent.

## Specific funder-rule tests (rather than guilt-by-association)

The [City 2025 ABF Guidelines](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/City-Hall/grants/Documents/2025%20Advance%20Brampton%20Fund/2025%20Advance%20Brampton%20Fund%20Guidelines%20V1.0.pdf), sections 8, 10–11, describe administrative eligibility, three evaluator scores, payment tranches, matching funds, and submission of original **itemized vendor invoices, proof of payment and participant/volunteer outcome records**. Nothing seen makes sharing directors, phone numbers, venues, or collaborating on programs automatically prohibited.

**Project test**: obtain each organization's exact City-approved activity title, dates, venue, participant counts, output commitments, event schedules and in-kind matching contributions for all six annual awards. Compare the proposed work **before** suggesting duplicate funding.

**Invoice/payment test**: compare grantee legal name and BN, actual grant-agreement signatory, original supplier invoices/invoice numbers, banking payee details to appropriate authorized reviewer, expense dates, receipt evidence, event venue deposits, performer payment logs and cost-sharing agreements. If both groups co-host an event, valid allocation/transparent cost sharing may be entirely permissible. Don't seek private bank details or participant identities unless authorized/legal and necessary.

**Governance/selection test**: did applications disclose a common decision-maker/project sponsor, and did different reviewer panels score the distinct projects? The City might have explicitly permitted both; internal records are not yet accessible. Avoid insinuating undisclosed conflicts without application forms.

**Independent delivery corroboration**: verify published theatre ticketing, dated performance programs and event venue records against each specific municipal-funded proposal, not random program marketing.

## Limits / exculpatory facts

- Both groups present a genuine public arts-program record, including older independently promoted shows; no fake or phantom programming has been established.
- Their distinct federal BNs mean **do not merge these into one legal entity solely because of overlapping directors/addresses**.
- Organizations can share board members, phone numbers, admin services and collaborative arts projects legally.
- **No original application, signed grant agreement, payment ledger, supplier invoice, expense worksheet, close-out or funder permission for shared expenses has been obtained**, so current findings are a **public-record reconciliation lead**, **not 'fraud risk' scoring**.
- The overlap of director name and address has been compared against sources, but source files do not establish when SSMLC governance began.
