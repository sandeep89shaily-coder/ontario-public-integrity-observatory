# Contractor performance research: public-interest evidence and limitations

Scope: **Mississauga, Toronto, Brampton**. The historical collector currently samples only Mississauga purchasing reports. This is a source-backed lead queue, **not** a fraud-detection result.

## First verified documentary lead: Mississauga / Mopal Construction

Official acquisition reports identify:
- December 2025: PRC005202, replacement of spray-pad surfaces, $910,600, [official report](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/12094038/December-2025-Competitive-Acquisitions-Report.pdf).
- July 2026: PRC005487, park trail resurfacing, $1,263,695, [official report](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/19144157/July-2026-Competitive-Acquisitions.pdf).
- August 2026: PRC005464, spray-pad and park improvements, $2,337,355, [official report](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/09/10154206/August-2026-Competitive-Acquisition-Report-Ext_1_1.pdf).
- September 2026: PRC005502, Sandgate Park, $323,130, [official report](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/09113345/September-2026-Competitive-Acquisitions-Report.pdf).
- October 2026: PRC005342, community gardens, $585,396; PRC005414, Max Ward mountain bike course, $343,750, [official report](https://www.mississauga.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/11/11081727/October-2026-Competitive-Acquisitions-Report.pdf).

**Reported six-entry acquisition total: $5,763,926**, not completed spending. Date is month of report, not necessarily contract award date. Some projects may be ongoing. The published values do not establish poor performance, overrun, favoritism or procurement problems.

The [City's archived pathways project page](https://www.mississauga.ca/projects-and-strategies/city-projects/building-our-future-mississauga/other-improvements/) names Mopal Construction among contractors and shows project-wide construction stages completed by 2011. This is evidence of a historical project mention and **city-reported project completion**, not a contractor-specific evaluation, budget reconciliation, or proof of legal-entity continuity.

## Known-case calibration: Toronto / Aplus

Official [2019 council decision record](https://secure.toronto.ca/council/agenda-item.do?item=2019.IE2.5) describes documented poor performance on a named contract and the City’s suspension decision. The [2022 council decision](https://secure.toronto.ca/council/report.do?meeting=2022.CC47&type=decisions) records a further three-year ineligibility decision. This is **publicly documented municipal action**, not a new allegation by the Observatory. Use it to test whether AI distinguishes a formal finding from an ordinary repeat award.

## Next evidence to acquire

For each procurement ID: executed contract (where disclosable), start/target completion, change-order log, final cost, deficiency log, substantial completion/acceptance certificate, vendor-performance evaluations, formal corrective action, bidder count/award rationale. Compare these against **later** award decision dates; don't assume the City could have known about a future problem.

Do not publish adverse contractor profiles until documentary corroboration, alternative explanations, legal/editorial review and appropriate right of reply.
