## What the organization publicly says it does

[Organization website](https://theskills4life.ca/), [programs](https://theskills4life.ca/programs/):
- **Discover Your Path:** school, afterschool and virtual life-skills sessions: SMART goals, financial/digital literacy, wellness and career planning.
- **New Age Mentorship:** one-to-one youth career/life mentorship, plus a program explicitly described as in collaboration with **Federation of Black Canadians (FBC)**: https://theskills4life.ca/new-age-mentorship/ .
- **Skills At Work:** youth employment and entrepreneurship incubator, hands-on landscaping and other work activities: https://theskills4life.ca/skills-at-work/ .
- [2024–25 impact report](https://theskills4life.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/Impact-Report-SFL-2024-25-compressed.pdf) reports **7 in-school cohorts, 62 in-school youth, 26 afterschool youth, 36 mentorship matches, 3 youth in paid landscaping work, 13 beautification contracts and 100 youth reached directly**. All are **recipient self-reports**, not independently verified receipts, participant counts or City-funded project acceptance.

## Related-party and intermediary relationships with documentary support

**FBC — formal disclosed related party.** The [2025 independently audited statement, note 6](https://theskills4life.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024-25-Audited-Financial-Statement-HRP-1-compressed.pdf), explicitly states:
- FBC made an **$80,000 unsecured, interest-free loan** to Skills For Life in the FY ended Aug 31, 2025;
- it was **$80,000 outstanding at that date**, repayable on Aug 31, 2025, and **fully repaid after year end** according to the auditor-disclosed note;
- common executive director **Chris Thompson** is the basis of the relationship.
- [University of Toronto public biography](https://www.utm.utoronto.ca/alumni/2024-alumni-mentorship-program-mentors) independently identifies Thompson's leadership roles at Skills For Life and FBC.
- The 2024 *unaudited* statements, note 5, report **$50,636 due to FBC** within funds held in trust. Distinguish this from the later $80,000 loan; these are differently classified balances. No direct evidence of public-grant diversion or misappropriation.

**Aspire 4 Higher — identified funds-held-in-trust counterparty.** The 2025 audit, note 5, reports **$12,500 due to Aspire 4 Higher** and describes holding funds in trust for other organizations. The [2024 City grant list](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/City-Hall/grants/Documents/2024%20Approved%20ABF%20Applicants%20%28For%20Web%29%20V.2.pdf) separately names **Aspire for Higher Elite Basketball** ($12,500); [2025 City list](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/City-Hall/grants/Documents/2025%20Advance%20Brampton%20Fund/2025%20Advance%20Brampton%20Fund%20Approved%20Applicants%20%28For%20Web%20V.%201%29.pdf) names **Aspire for Higher Sports** ($8,750), and [2026 City list](https://www.brampton.ca/EN/City-Hall/grants/Documents/2026%20Advance%20Brampton%20Fund/2026-Advance-Brampton-Fund-Approved-Recipients.pdf) names **Aspire for Higher** ($20,000). [A4H's own page](https://aspireforhigher.ca/impact/) describes related brand/subsidiary names. **Legal identity, trust beneficiary, grant money provenance and the source of the $12,500 are NOT reconciled.** The identical 2024 amount is not proof the held funds were a Brampton grant. Requires contract and fund-source records if accessible.
