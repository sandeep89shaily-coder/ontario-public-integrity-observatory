# CASE 002 — Aspire for Higher: deeper funding, decision and delivery investigation

**Investigation updated 2026-11-20. Status:** public-source due diligence. **No identified fraud, misappropriation, preferential treatment, or conflict of interest.** This document does **not** accuse Aspire for Higher or its leadership of misconduct. ## Scope: what the investigation can actually prove

The focal entity is Aspire for Higher Elite Basketball, federal public-funding recipient BN **817694599**, using the brand/operating name Aspire for Higher. Federal [agreement 1000102 amendment](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/record/feddevontario%2C062-2022-2023-Q2-00175%2Ccurrent) explicitly notes the 2022 operating-name change. The City's 2024–26 records have several printed versions, which are plausible branded program names, but **signed city agreements have not been reviewed to unify their legal recipients**.

**Do not call the federal recipient a CRA-registered charity merely because the federal database categorizes it as 'not-for-profit organization or charity'.** Registration status must be separately validated through a dated official CRA record.

## Key discovery: a precise SDF program with potential co-funding to reconcile

1. An [official Ministry SDF Round 4 approved-project list](https://eopg.labour.gov.on.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/09/sdf-round-4-organization-list-en-1.pdf) names **Aspire For Higher Elite Basketball — 'Extra Judicial Skilled Trades Employment Fast Track Program'**. The PDF does NOT display dollars, trainee targets, score or invoice data.
2. [The Trillium, October 31, 2025](https://www.thetrillium.ca/news/politics/most-early-skills-development-fund-recipients-that-missed-a-key-training-or-employment-target-got-grants-again-11428916), reported that the minister's office awarded Aspire **$4.5 million** to provide home-building training and quotes a program participant describing a later full-time carpentry job. The amount is **journalistically reported; the signed $4.5m transfer-payment agreement has not been obtained**, and the article's broader discussion of poor SDF outcomes must not be ascribed to Aspire without its individual KPI record.
3. The [Ontario 2024–25 Public Accounts](https://www.ontario.ca/files/2025-09/tbs-public-detailed-schedule-of-payments-2024-2025-en-fr-2025-09-26.pdf) show **$1,773,039** paid/listed to *Aspire For Higher Elite Basketball* under **Ministry of Labour, Immigration, Training and Skills Development** transfer payments (printed page 206). This is **fiscal-year payment data**, not confirmation that the amount is exclusively from the $4.5m award. Public Accounts does not expose monthly transactions, signed project budget or performance milestones.
4. [United Way Greater Toronto's 2025 project listing](https://www.unitedwaygt.org/our-work/investments/community-action-grants/) independently names Aspire as the agency leading an **'Extra-Judicial Employment Fast Track'** Cooksville project for justice-involved youth learning carpentry, electrical or plumbing work. Its [2025–26 published disbursement/allocations table](https://annualreport.unitedwaygt.org/disbursements.php) lists **$75,000** for an Aspire Cooksville Community Action Grant. The funder's agency-led grant cap is $75,000. The similar project names are an **overlap requiring cost-allocation verification, not evidence of a duplicate claim**. One may be an incremental place-based cohort or different expenses.
5. Aspire's [2024 impact report](https://aspireforhigher.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/2024-A4H-Impact-Report_final.pdf) self-reports **52 graduates, 34 jobs created** from its 12-week EJEFT program, while describing **31 adults in job placements/14,880 placement hours** in its wider skills-lab section. In 2025 the [impact report](https://aspireforhigher.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/2025-A4H-Impact-Report.pdf) reports **28 pre-apprenticeship graduates, 23 job placements, 5,000+ placement hours**, and 2025 grocery/housing support described as $12,400 and $117,080.34. **These are non-identical periods and likely different measurement populations; do not infer performance fell from 52 to 28.** Web screenshot retrieval of the large 2024 PDF failed owing to file size and 2025 PDF retrieval returned 404 during this run, so the indexed PDF text/claims require visual confirmation before adverse use.
6. We need the individual SDF **Transfer Payment Agreement** (TPA), initial score and evaluator comments, ministry/minister selection notes, signed amount and amendments, approved budget categories, baseline+final trainee and employment KPI schedules, actual program reports, expenditures, final financial audit/certification, and any holdbacks/recoveries. We also need United Way's separately approved activity budget and grants expenditure list to determine whether they covered distinct costs or legitimately co-financed the same delivery.

**Important timing:** The 2024–25 payment amount, 2024 self-report, 2025 United Way grant, 2025 report and 2026 announcement cannot be directly aggregated into one project or fiscal-year finding.

## 2025 Auditor General SDF findings and how to use them

[Office of the Auditor General of Ontario, Oct 2025, Performance Audit of SDF Training Stream](https://www.auditor.on.ca/en/content/specialreports/specialreports/en25/AR-PA_SDF-TS_en25.pdf) finds documented *system-wide* issues with the selection and approval process and differences in applicant-negotiated performance targets. It describes project TPAs, reporting requirements, final-cost reconciliation, recovery criteria and generally up to 15% holdback. It finds that 708 projects in Rounds 1–4 achieved 64% of combined target for previously unemployed participants employed 60 days after completing programs.

**These findings are NOT findings about Aspire for Higher's particular selection score, milestone success, or government relationships.** Obtain its actual TPA and original ranking before discussing this organization's selection. Do not infer that it was among low-scoring recipients or had missing reports.

## Additional federal funding and program legitimacy

- [FedDev Agreement **1000102**, $977,070](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/record/feddevontario%2C062-2021-2022-Q2-00108%2Ccurrent) covers the earlier 2021–2024 Black Health Professionals Network (BHPN). The [2022 amendment listing](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/record/feddevontario%2C062-2022-2023-Q2-00175%2Ccurrent) shows **the exact same agreement ID and amount** — **NOT a second $977,070 award**. This is a mandatory deduplication test.
- [FedDev agreement **1019626**, $2,800,000](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/?search_text=%22Aspire+for+Higher+Elite+Basketball%22), April 1 2026 through Dec 31 2029, funds healthcare business training, digital support, incubator and advisory work. The [August 26 2026 announcement](https://www.canada.ca/en/economic-development-southern-ontario/news/2026/08/nine-organizations-receive-support-to-strengthen-opportunities-for-black-led-businesses-and-entrepreneurs-across-southern-ontario.html) confirms the face value, **not disbursements already made**. The older BHPN record gives relevant experience and a plausible rationale for a related healthcare entrepreneurship program; ask how project milestones differ and what the first project achieved.
- Federal [public search](https://search.open.canada.ca/grants/?search_text=%22Aspire+for+Higher+Elite+Basketball%22) returns **67 name-matched records**, of which **56 ESDC, 3 FedDev Ontario and 8 PHAC**; many appear to be separate location/riding-based **Canada Summer Jobs** agreements. This search count is NOT 67 independent proven payments and is NOT an allegation of payroll duplication. Individual CSJ agreements have different agreement IDs; payroll, worksite and youth employment claims would be needed to reconcile them.
- **Substantive independent positive corroboration**: a [2026 Public Health Agency of Canada community report](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2026/aspc-phac/HP35-198-2026-eng.pdf) reports Aspire's 2021–2024 mental-wellness basketball initiative reached **423 youth**, and trained **23 facilitators**. This supports evidence of real programming rather than an ungrounded 'shell organization' theory. It does not validate SDF or ABF project invoices.

## Relationship and governance question: Skills For Life Inc.

[Skills For Life Inc's independently audited 2025 financial statements](https://theskills4life.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2024-25-Audited-Financial-Statement-HRP-1-compressed.pdf), **note 5**, show **$12,500 due to 'Aspire 4 Higher'** as funds held in trust for other organizations, with administration fees tracked separately. This is a real **documented inter-organization financial relationship**, but only at the named beneficiary level. It is **not** proof that the amount came from Aspire's **same-dollar 2024 Brampton ABF grant**. Obtain the trust agreement and distinct legal beneficiary identifier; compare to source-of-funds documents before drawing inferences. Note 6 discloses a separate **$80,000 related-party loan from the Federation of Black Canadians** to Skills For Life and a shared director there; this is NOT evidence that FBC lent money to Aspire or that Aspire's board overlaps.

Aspire's own current [board page](https://aspireforhigher.ca/board-of-directors/) names Nicole Morris (chair), Roya Assadi (treasurer), Dr. Nikolai Whyte and Nicolette Stewart (board), with Abena Addo identified in organizational reports as founder/executive director. Board history differs in older reports; **no latest corporate filing obtained**, and no personal or political relationship inferred.
